# The signatory attests at approval and an operator files the notice

A notice is a statement made under penalty of perjury, and the recipient forms ask for a typed full legal name as the signature. Patrol separates the person who attests from the person who operates the form: the **signatory** is the natural person the rights holder authorised, snapshotted into the notice at approval and rendered in every identity and signature field; the **operator** who approves and the operator who files are recorded on Patrol's side and never typed into a recipient's form. Approval is the attestation; filing is the delivery.

## Considered options

- **The filing operator signs.** Makes the person at the keyboard the person under the statement, which is truthful only when the operator is the signatory, and produces a signature that must match a different name at the top of Google's form.
- **Only the signatory may file.** Cleanest identity story, and it makes the signatory the throughput ceiling: every notice waits for one person to sit in front of three forms.
- **The signatory attests per notice in a separate signing step.** Adds an explicit signature ceremony, but the notice is already approved by a person and the recipient's form is where the real signature is entered, so the step duplicates without strengthening.
- **Patrol signs on the rights holder's behalf.** Not a choice: a machine cannot make the sworn statement, and a typed name the signatory never approved is a misrepresentation.

## Consequences

- The notice records one signatory at approval, and the submission records the operator who filed; the two records never merge, and the same person may hold both.
- The handoff pack renders the signatory's full legal name and the rights holder's role mailbox, never an operator's personal details, because Google republishes notices through Lumen and requires the signature to match the name at the top of the form.
- The operator's attestation in the pack is about delivery — that the notice was filed as presented — not about the ownership claim, which the signatory already attested.
- A signatory change takes effect for new notices only; approved notices and their submissions keep the signatory they were snapshot with.
- Pressing throughput, the signatory is a profile rather than an account, so the rights holder can name a new signatory without provisioning a login.
