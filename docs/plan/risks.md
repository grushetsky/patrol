# Open risks

The risks a build effort inherits, each with the slice that de-risks it. A residual risk is one the build cannot remove; it is accepted or watched, not solved.

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation | De-risking slice | Residual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Provider list prices change | A 20% increase cuts daily capacity from about 150 titles to about 120 at the same cap. | Re-quote before build and quarterly; the capacity projection derives from price, not a stored row. | 3, 8 | The cap binds sooner; the rights holder lowers cadence or the locale set. |
| Google's per-file URL cap is unpublished | A batch larger than the form accepts is refused at filing. | Default to 500 URLs, lower to the last observed cap, learn only from the form's error text, never hard-code. | 6 | Discovery of a lower cap delays one batch. |
| Provider balance or credentials fail | Scans stop for that engine, and work queues without explanation. | Daily balance probe, route disable on authentication failure, hourly recovery probe, alerts naming the engine and bucket. | 3 | Patrol cannot scan until the provider is topped up. |
| Yandex refuses a non-eligible rights holder | Submission is impossible for a US, EU, or Swiss rights holder only. | Eligibility is a profile predicate; the ineligible profile cannot be handed off and shows the reason; discovery stays on. | 6 | Yandex submission is unavailable for other jurisdictions. |
| A host blocks or objects to capture | The page cannot be evidenced, and a candidate may have no capture at review. | Record `blocked`, retry inside 24 hours, record the objection, suspend the host, surface affected candidates to an operator; never rotate identity or solve challenges. | 4 | Some candidates stay unevidenced and cannot become notices. |
| Object Lock is unavailable or weaker than expected | Evidence immutability rests on policy rather than storage. | Compliance mode in production, governance mode in development, version-pinned reads and deletes, hashes in two places. | 4, 9 | Development guarantees are weaker than production. |
| The capture posture is challenged legally | A host or recipient disputes the fetch. | Single-URL fetch, identifiable user agent, no login or circumvention, allowlist before fetch, objection handling, documented reasoning in ADR 0018. | 4, 9 | Legal review remains an operational dependency. |
| Matching admits a false positive | A notice claims a copy that is not one, under a perjury statement, republished by Lumen. | Human review for every violation, allowlists, reputation only alongside a signal, the first-five second-operator check, rejection threshold pause. | 5, 6 | Review is the control; a bad decision remains possible. |
| Matching misses a copy | Coverage falls silently. | Full-plan re-checks, fixture-based precision and recall tests, manual URL entry, queue ordering that surfaces strong evidence first. | 5 | No search-based monitor finds everything. |
| Re-check cost starves discovery | Verification consumes the month before new scans run. | The 30% re-check share is not borrowable; re-checks starve first; alerts at 75, 90, and 100%; priority degradation. | 3, 8 | A high count of open violations can exhaust the re-check share. |
| Submission is the throughput ceiling | Approved notices wait in the operator's queue. | Seven-day reminders, attempt flags, a single-row retry, first-five second-operator review; the Trusted Copyright Removal Program is pursued, not depended on. | 7, 9 | Filing remains manual by design. |
| Lumen republishes the signatory's name and contact | Personal data becomes public and cannot be undone. | The pack states the republication; no operator name, personal email, phone, or home address reaches a form. | 6, 9 | The signatory must accept public attribution. |
| TOTP is phishable in real time | An operator's session could be taken over. | TOTP plus password, short sessions, revocation on change, recovery codes; passkeys are a later credential type. | 1 | Real-time phishing is not prevented. |
| The bootstrap token appears in container logs | Anyone with log access can claim the first administrator. | Single-use token, setup refused once a rights holder exists, log retention review, documented handling. | 1, 9 | Log access remains privileged. |
| The last administrator is locked out | No administrator can sign in. | `patrol admin recover` on the host, runbook, warning before disabling the only other administrator. | 1, 9 | Host access becomes the recovery path. |
| A restore drill is skipped | The four-hour RTO becomes an aspiration. | Quarterly drill is an acceptance criterion, not a suggestion. | 9 | An unrehearsed restore can exceed the target. |
| A purge runs while a legal hold applies | Spoliation exposure. | Holds reach bucket lifecycle, closure deletion, and shared-blob cleanup; the purge job tests every path. | 9 | A mis-scoped hold can still miss a record. |
| Concurrent edits have no optimistic control | Two operators can race a transition. | Server state guards and unique constraints answer `409`; the contract declares no `If-Match`; a build that needs more records a new decision. | 5, 6, 7 | Last-writer-wins remains possible for non-transitional edits. |
| Recipient statement texts are undecided | Notice composition cannot resolve the current statements. | The contract carries a statement version and resolved fields; the exact texts and their versioning must be decided before slice 6 ships. | 6 | Blocks notice composition until decided. |
| Administrator screens are unspecified | Rights holder, recipient profiles, budget, operators, and legal holds have APIs but no prototype. | Design them from the contract and the design documents in slice 9; the API constrains the fields. | 9 | UI design effort is not estimated by the prototype. |
| The design-point capacity assumption is wrong | 150 titles in two locales is a planning number, not a measured one. | Load-test at the design point during slice 3 and recompute capacity from the current cap and prices. | 3, 8 | The cap may need renegotiation or a cadence change. |
